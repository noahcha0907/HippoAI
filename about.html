About Us

Make clinical AI easier to repair.

We’re building Hippo around a focused question: can we repair a specific failure inside a small healthcare AI model, then explain the change in terms that matter to people?

Why Hippo.

Healthcare AI can fail in subtle ways. A model may follow a misleading suggestion, rely on a shortcut, or make an error whose importance is hidden by an overall accuracy score.

Our aim is to connect the technical work of finding and editing weights with the practical work of validating a repair. A better score is useful only when we understand what improved, what worsened, and what still needs review.

What we’re
building toward.

A research pipeline for teams developing or evaluating small healthcare AI models.

Focused repairs

Investigate a known failure and change the weights most closely connected to it. Keep a record of each edit so it can be reviewed and reversed.

Independent evidence

Evaluate on tests the editing process did not use. Compare against the original model and conventional methods, and check for new failures.

Useful explanations

Report what changed in the model’s behavior, how that could affect a clinical workflow, and where uncertainty remains.

Patient impact
comes first.

Technical metrics tell us how often a model is wrong. They do not, on their own, tell us what those errors mean for a patient.

Our proposed reports follow three questions: how many errors occurred, what was missed, and what could change in care? Clinical interpretation belongs with the people who understand that care pathway.

The clinical-impact framework ↗

Research,
with clear limits.

Hippo is a research project. The published studies motivating our work are not results from our own pipeline, and weight editing is not a certification of clinical safety.

Our initial hypothesis is that targeted editing could reduce sycophancy: a model’s tendency to agree with a user even when the user is wrong. That repair still needs to be demonstrated.
